{ Summary

Correlation : Simultaneous change in the values of two variables and direct or indirect

cause-effect relationship between them.

Linear Correlation : There are almost constant proportional changes in the values of two
variables i.e. the points corresponding to the values of two correlated variables are on or
nearer to a line.

Positive Correlation : The changes in the values of two correlated variables are in the same direction.
Negative Correlation : The changes in the values of two correlated variables are
in the opposite direction.

Correlation Coefficient : The numerical measure showing the strength of linear correlation
between two variables is a correlation coefficient.

Scatter diagram : A simple method for identifying linear correlation and its type (positive
or negative).

Karl Pearson’s Method : The best method of obtaining type and strength of linear correlation
using all observations.

Spearman’s Rank Correlation Method : A method for obtaining the correlation coefficient
for qualitative variables and also preferable when dispersion is more in quantitative variables.
The cause and effect relation between two variables cannot be proved but under the assumption
that it does exist, the concept of correlation is studied.

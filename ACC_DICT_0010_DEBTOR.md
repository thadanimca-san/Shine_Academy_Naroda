Definition

A Debtor is a person, business, or organization that owes money to the business because goods or services have been supplied on credit.

Simple Explanation

Suppose Rahul owns a stationery shop.

A school purchases notebooks worth ₹15,000 but promises to pay after 30 days.

Until payment is received, the school is Rahul's Debtor.

The school owes money to Rahul.

Therefore, it is recorded as a Debtor.

Universal Concept

Credit sales are common throughout the world.

Businesses often allow trusted customers to pay later.

Such customers become Debtors until payment is received.

Accounting Perspective

Debtors are Current Assets because they are expected to convert into cash within the normal operating cycle.

Examples

• Customer purchasing goods on credit

• School buying books on credit

• Hospital receiving medicines on credit

• Company supplying goods with 45-day payment terms

Importance

Debtors help increase sales but also create collection risk.

Efficient debtor management improves business cash flow.

Common Mistakes

❌ Every customer is a Debtor.

✔ Only customers who purchase on credit become Debtors.

Teacher Tip

Ask students:

"If your friend borrows ₹500 from you and promises to return it next week, who owes whom?"

Then relate this idea to business credit sales.

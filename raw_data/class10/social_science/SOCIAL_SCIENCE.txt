10. Write notes on types of agriculture.

Ans. Ans. There are mainly six types of agriculture In India: 1. Subsistence farming, 2. Dry farming, 3. Wet farming, 4.
Shifting (Jhoom) agriculture, 5. Plantation agriculture and 6. Intensive farming. 1. Subsistence farming : That cultivation
wherein the agricultural production is consumed by the family is called ‘Subsistence farming’. In most of India today
subsistence farming Is the main activity for the maintenance.
Most of the Indian farmers have smaller size of farms, few have only stray pieces of land and have lesser facility for
irrigation.
They cannot afford the use of modern farm implements, costly seeds, fertilizers and insecticides due to their poverty.
Farm production is just enough for the use of the family so It Is used away in the maintenance of the family. That is why
it is known as subsistence farming.
In most of India today subsistence farming is the main activity for the maintenance. 2. Dry farming: ‘Dry farming’ is
practiced In the regions where rainfall is less, Irrigation facilities are also less and the agriculture Is dependent only on
rainfall.
As the cultivation here depends only on the humidity retained in the soil, only one crop can be taken during the year.
Crops like jowar, millet and pulses are taken.
Wheat and gram are grown in the humid soil of Bhal region of Gujarat after the monsoon ends. 3. Wet farming: ‘The Wet
farming’ Is practiced in the regions where there is sufficient rainfall and has the irrigation facility.
When there Is no rainfall or if it Is insufficient, then more than one crop can be taken with the help of Irrigation.
Paddy, sugarcane, cotton, wheat, vegetables, etc. are grown In this soil. 4. Shifting (Jhoom) agriculture : This type of
cultivation is practiced in dense forests.
Here the forests are destroyed or burnt to clear the land for agriculture.
After two — three years when the soil fertility Is reduced, the area is abandoned, people migrate to another area and
agriculture is practiced In the same manner there. This Is called ‘Jhoom Cultivation’.
Mostly foodgrains and vegetables are grown here.
Per hectare production in such farming is less. 5. Plantation agriculture : Big gardens and orchards are used to raise

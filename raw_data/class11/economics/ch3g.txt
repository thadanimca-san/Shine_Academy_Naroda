( 7 ) Why is there inverse relationship
between price and demand ?
Ans. If all other factors remain constant,
there is extension of demand as the price falls.
The reasons for this inverse relation between
the price and demand are : (1) New consumers
find the commodity affordable; hence, number of
consumers increases, and (2) the old consumers
are induced to buy more.

( 1 ) At a certain price, some of the consumers
with low income could not afford to buy the
commodity. As the price falls, they are inclined
to buy and therefore demand expands.
( 2 ) Those consumers who were buying a
certain quantity of the commodity at a higher
price find that they can afford to buy more at
a lower price. This happens due to two kinds of
effects : ( i ) Substitution effect and (ii) Income effect.
( i ) Substitution effect : Tea and coffee are
substitutes of each other. If the price of tea falls
and the price of coffee remains the same, people
who are using coffee will now turn to tea. The
commodity whose price has fallen becomes cheaper
as compared to its substitute and therefore demand
for it would increase. Increase in demand for tea
will be at the cost of coffee, i.e., demand for
coffee would decrease.

( ii ) Income effect : If the price of a commodity
falls, the real income of the consumer increases.
This leads to increase in demand for the
commodity. In economics, this is called the 'income
effect.' e.g., suppose the money income of a family
is constant and the family spends t 90 on buying
3 kgs of rice at t 30 per kg. Now suppose the
price of rice falls to t 25 and the family buys
the same amount of rice, i.e., 3 kgs. The total
expenditure will be ~ 75. There is a clear gain
of ~ 15 . to the family on account of fall in price
of rice. Therefore the family will now buy a little
more rice than before, say 3.5 kgs. This is the
income effect.

Because of the combined substitution and
income effects, an inverse relation between price
and demand is established. The demand curve is
downward sloping.

7. INDIAN ECONOMY

* Indian Economy before Independenc e :
• Before independence during the British rule
th e EaS t 1ndia Company and later the western
rulers came to India for the purpose of doing
business.
• India was famous for trade in cotton, muslin,
indigo, spices and metallurgy.
• During this time there were conflicts among
the foreign rulers to establish their own power
in India.
• When the British established dominance, our
resources were exploited and the ancient
riches of India started eroding.
• However, Indian economy attained the following :
( 1 ) Railway :

• British set up the railway infrastructure
in India and the first railway was started
between Boribandar (present CST) and
Thane on 16th April, 1853.
• By 1947, India had rail network of
53000 km benefiting a population of
68 lakhs.
( 2 ) Roadways :

• The British also created a good length
of roads in India by setting up the
Public Works Department (PWD).
• By the end of 19th century, there were
a total of 2, 78,420 km of roads which
increased to 4,47,105 km of which 32 %
were concrete roads.
(3)Banks:

• Private sector banks were started in
1770.
• Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was set
up in 1935.
• By 1946, there were more than 700
banks in India.
( 4 ) Social Structure :
• Certain wrong social practices like
prevention of female infanticide, sati
pratha, etc. were abolished during the

British rule.
( 5 ) Agriculture :
• Because of high revenue, Zamindari
system and other acts of the British,
Indian farmers were exploited very much

and they became poor.

• After industrial revolution in Britain,
Indian farmers were compelled to
grow more indigo , but the British
government did not waive off their loan
and the farmers became debtor. Thus,
it detoriated the conditiqn of Indian
farmers during the British rule.
( 6 ) Land Revenue :
• Land revenues were as high as half of
produces of the farmers.
• The British government collected land
revenue from the farmers through the
Zamindars.
• The farmers who failed to give revenue
were punished by the government and
were charged fines.
( 7 ) High rates of Taxes :
• Indians who were poor, had to pay
15 % of the national income as taxes.
( 8 ) High rates of Excise and Customs :
• Excise rates were high on commodities
like sugar, matches, steel, silver, etc.
• Though salt was easily available in
India, the British monopolised salt trade
and imported salt to India by levying
high custom duties and thus made salt
expensive for poor Indians. To protest
this, Gandhiji started the salt satyagrah
with the Dandi March.
• 15 % excise were imposed on Indian
cotton cloth exports, while import tariffs
of only about 2.5 % was on the cotton
cloth imports from England.
( 9 ) Industrial Policy :

• The period from 1750 to 1830 was
the period of industrial revolution and
by that time East India Company had
started establishing rule in India.
• In 1858, the British government
established its rule. The British
government adopted a policy such
that only light and consumeries were
started by Indian industries and no
attention was given to other basic and
key industries.
• The industrial policy adopted by the
British government was such that · to
export raw material from India to Britain
and sell goods made in Britain to India.

* Indian Economy after Independence : After
independence (15th August, 1947), the changes
created in Indian economy are as follows :

( 1 ) Per Capita Income :
• According to Human Development Report
2014, the per capita income of India in
2013, was $ 5150.
• It is lower than that of developed countries
(USA, England, Germany, Norway) but
also lower than many developing countries
like China, Sri Lanka.

( 2 ) Agriculture :

• The British made only those investment
in India, which benefitted more to
Britain.

• At the time of independence 72 % of the
population was dependent on agriculture
which declined to 58 % in 2001 - 02 and
to 49 % in 2013- 14.

• The British invested in railways and
road networks to help movement of raw
materials and goods for their industries.

• In 1950 - 51, the share of agriculture in
national income was 55 % which declined
to only 14 % in 2013- 14.

• They invested in education to run
the British administration. The British
prevented Indians from getting technical
education for their benefit.
• They made selective investments in India
only for their benefit.

(13) Payment burden:
• India had to pay a huge amount of
money known as 'Home Charges' towards .
maintenance of British colony.
• Horne charges included salaries of British
personnel, administration, maintenance
of the army, war expenses, pensions
to retired officers, interest payments
for debts, interest on the railway, civil
and military charge, etc.
• In 1945, the amount of home charges
was 1350 lakhs pound. Thus, due to
various payments, the British became
prosperous.
• Summery :
➔ During

the British rule, India was
under much pressure in agriculture,
industry and trade sectors.

• Thus, after independence, the importance
of agriculture is reducing.

( 3 ) Industries :
• The share of industrial sector in
employment was 10.6 % in 1950-51.
which increased to 18.8 % in 2001 and
further to 24.3 % in 2011 - 12.
• The share of industrial sector in national
income was 16.6 % in 1950 - 51 and it
was 26 % in 2013- 14.
• Thus, after independence the industrial
sector is progressing but cannot be called
a completely industrialised nation.
( 4 ) Service Sector :

• The share of service sector in employment
was 17.3 % in 1951, which increased to
25.2 % in 2011-12.
• The share of service sector in national
income was 30.3 % in 1951 which
increased to 50.4 % in 2000- 01 and
further to 52.7% in 2014-15.
• Thus, it shows that India is moving fast
on the path of growth and development.

( 5 ) Population Growth :
• The population in India was 36.1 crores
in 1951 which increased to 102.7 crores
in 2001 and further to 121.02 crores
in 2011.
• Thus, after independence, the growth rate
of population in India is 1.8 % annually.
Such a high rate of growth of population
has remained a matter of worry.
( 6 ) Poverty :
• The population living below poverty line
was 54.9 % in 1973 - 74 which declined
to 37.2 % in 2004- 05 and further to
21.9% in 2011-12.
• Thus, after independence, even though the
population living below poverty line has
declined over years, the Indian economy
is characterised by high levels of absolute
poverty.
( 7 ) Unemployment :
• In 1951, 33 lakhs people were
unemployed, which increased to 8.2 %
in 2004 - 05. However, this decreased to
6.6 % in 2009 - 10 and further to 5.6 %
in 2011 - 12.
• Thus, after independence, unemployment
has been reduced in India but in the
rural areas, the proportion of disguised
unemployment is higher.
( 8 ) Human development :
• Human development Index of India
was 0.463 in 2000, which was 0.54 7
in 2010, 0.554 in 2012 and 0.586 in
2013.
• Thus, after independence, HDI of India
is considered very low and India stands
at 136th rank in a list of 187 countries.

Definition

The Double Entry System is a method of accounting in which every financial transaction affects at least two accounts, ensuring that the accounting equation always remains balanced.

--------------------------------------------------

Simple Explanation

Imagine you buy a laptop for your business.

You pay ₹50,000 in cash.

Two things happen:

1. Laptop (Asset) increases.
2. Cash (Asset) decreases.

One transaction affects two accounts.

That is the Double Entry System.

--------------------------------------------------

Why Was It Invented?

Before this system, merchants often forgot transactions or made mistakes.

Around the 15th century, the Italian mathematician Luca Pacioli documented the principles of double-entry bookkeeping, making accounting more reliable.

Today, almost every business in the world uses this system.

--------------------------------------------------

Golden Rule

Every Debit has an equal Credit.

--------------------------------------------------

Accounting Equation

Assets = Capital + Liabilities

Every transaction must keep this equation balanced.

--------------------------------------------------

Advantages

• Complete record of transactions.

• Easy detection of mistakes.

• Preparation of Trial Balance.

• Reliable Financial Statements.

• Accepted throughout the world.

--------------------------------------------------

Disadvantages

• Requires accounting knowledge.

• Time-consuming for beginners.

• Complex for very small businesses.

--------------------------------------------------

Real-Life Example

A supermarket records thousands of transactions every day.

Every sale,

every purchase,

every salary,

every electricity bill,

is recorded using the Double Entry System.

Without it, the business would not know its actual profit or financial position.

--------------------------------------------------

Teacher Tip

Use two buckets filled with water.

Whenever water is removed from one bucket, it is added to another.

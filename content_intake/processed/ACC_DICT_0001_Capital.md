# Definition
## Beginner
Suppose Priya wants to open a bookstore. She brings: ₹8,00,000 Cash, Furniture worth ₹75,000, Computer worth ₹45,000. The total value of everything she contributes becomes her Capital. Without capital, a business cannot begin.

## Intermediate
Capital is the total amount of money or other assets introduced by the owner into the business for starting or expanding business operations. It represents the owner's financial interest in the business.

## Advanced
Every business requires resources to operate. These resources usually come from the owner or investors. The owner's contribution is called Capital. This follows the Business Entity Concept where Capital is treated as a liability because the business owes this amount to its owner.

# Real Life Examples
- A doctor starts a clinic. She invests: Cash ₹15,00,000, Medical Equipment ₹8,00,000, Furniture ₹2,00,000. Total Capital = ₹25,00,000

# Importance
## Accounting
- It is the foundation of accounting
- Affects Owner's Equity
- Affects Balance Sheet (Liability Side)
- Required for Partnership Accounts and Company Accounts

# Teacher Notes
## Misconception
Wrong: Capital always means Cash. Correct: Capital may include Cash, Furniture, Land, Building, Machinery, Computer, Inventory, Vehicles. Any asset invested by the owner.
